# A7 空間工作室

Static browser-based Three.js interior editor. The complete public output is `dist/`.

Run `node server.mjs` to preview at http://127.0.0.1:4173. Run `npm test` for model geometry / import checks. No build is required. All rendering dependencies are vendored with the Three.js MIT licence.

## Current editor capabilities

- Orbit overview, top-down editing and first-person indoor navigation.
- Add, rename, duplicate, resize, rotate and move furniture; top-view edge dragging resizes an object while preserving the opposite edge. With the plan-ratio lock ticked, both typed and dragged width/depth changes scale the other plan dimension too; the lock starts unticked, then keeps the last choice across items and reloads.
- Hanging cabinets hang from the ceiling or from a beam wholly above them and take the same layouts, fronts and finishes as floor cabinets. Ceiling items stack ceiling → beam → hanging cabinet → light: a light under a cabinet is pushed down to its underside, and anything only partly under the item above is flagged. Floor furniture clashes with a hanging cabinet only where their heights overlap.
- New storage and TV cabinets start from a preset layout, then everything is edited cell by cell in an elevation view (there is no template picker): layers, shelf heights, open niches, bottom clearance and fronts; any single cell can be split again, side by side or top to bottom, to any depth. Adjacent cells that make a rectangle can share one hinged door (Shift/Ctrl-click or the multi-select switch, then merge), whose edges line up with the cells; drawers and sliding doors stay single-cell. Every door and drawer front can show a handle; handles are off by default. A cabinet made before the editor keeps its door style when first edited. Existing consoles can be converted without losing their TV: the TV becomes a separate object that can mount on a wall, hang inside an open cabinet cell, or stand on a cabinet's top or an open cell's shelf (it follows the cabinet; one too big for its place is flagged, not resized). New TVs default to the open cell they fit whose centre is nearest seated eye level, and a size menu (43 to 85 inch) sets width and height, which stay editable. Finishes are layered: the whole cabinet, then all doors, shelves, backs or drawer boxes, then any single cell's door, shelf, back or drawer box; each level follows the one above until set. The editor window floats without a backdrop, can be dragged and folded, so the cabinet stays in view while it changes. Its outer edges can be dragged: the sides, plus the top of a floor cabinet or the underside of a hanging one. Only the column or cells on that side change and the opposite edge stays where it is; a drag that would clash stops at the largest clear size and says why. Deleting a floor cabinet's top cell (a hanging cabinet's bottom cell) or an end column removes just that part instead of stretching its neighbours.
- Light coves (燈槽): a board on the wall a set distance below the ceiling (25 cm by default) with an LED strip on top. The wash on the ceiling and the wall above is painted as an additive gradient rather than an area light, plus one weak unshadowed lamp per cove, so coves never take a shadow slot. Colour temperature, lumens, dimming and switch follow the light fields; the wash is strongest at night or with the main lights off. Coves are straight segments (use two at a corner) and clash with tall furniture, hanging cabinets, lights and beams that reach down past them.
- A TV wall is built from ordinary furniture: a back panel (背板, a thin board with a finish and a height above the floor), a TV cabinet, hanging cabinets and a TV, each moved, resized, typed and finished like any other item. A wall-hung TV may hang on a back panel. Layouts saved with the old composite TV-wall object load as those separate items; its construction notes (outlets, conduits, access openings, site records) were dropped on 2026-09-26, since the plan is not a construction document.
- A7 exterior walls are hard movement boundaries. Interior collisions remain editable drafts so overlapping objects can be brought to the foreground and moved apart; table and desk tops allow chairs to tuck underneath while protecting legs and backs.
- Fixed doors, curtains, cabinet fronts, washer doors and both shower doors can be opened for a simplified sweep/interference check. Door leaves fill their frames and turn about a hinge on the swing-side wall face, so the hinge edge never moves. Walking passes through plants.
- Typed width/depth changes grow away from a wall or furniture the item already touches instead of being rejected.
- Ceiling lights come as flush round/square fittings, pendants or recessed linear lights (2 cm minimum width; their height is how far they drop below the ceiling, 1 cm minimum), with white/natural/warm colour temperature, lumens, dimming and pendant drop. Flush and linear fittings light downward only; switched-on lamps cast shadows, up to a GPU-safe budget of the nearest eight. Beams can cross interior partitions; lights report wall overlap. A light whose whole footprint lies under a beam is mounted on the beam's underside instead of clashing with it. Ceiling-object interference is shown as an editable draft, like floor furniture interference. Furniture that would stop a room door opening (anything below the 2.1 m door height inside its swing) is flagged the same way. A light that reaches floor furniture (a long pendant over a table, or any light pushed down by a beam or hanging cabinet above it) clashes with it, just as a hanging cabinet does.
- 54 board finishes modelled on a supplier catalogue (colours measured, textures generated in code) can be applied to furniture's wooden parts (not sofas, whose only wood is hidden legs). Floors take, room by room, one of 55 SPC floorings from a separate flooring catalogue: straight planks in two sizes, herringbone, and stone-look tiles, generated plank by plank with staggered joints. Sofas, chairs and beds take an upholstery colour: 11 presets or any colour.
- The object list filters by room and by kind. The reference drawings show their plan areas and open full size.
- Walk view looks out on the neighbouring towers (north across the lane, east next door, modelled on 2025 Street View), the street below and the sky, and through the front door onto the lift lobby of the 5/7/9/11F plan. The north tower uses recessed balconies, projecting stone piers, bronze louvres and a horizontal roof canopy with real elliptical openings. The right tower has a centred ring-and-louvre band on its alley-facing side wall, flanked by two stacks of windows and projecting floor blades. The east neighbour has a darker central tiled band, pale flanking tiles, one slit-window column, three exhaust-fitting columns and a recessed platform stack visible when looking right from the work balcony. Night darkens the sky and lights some windows.
- Local named scenarios can be saved, loaded and deleted. The current working layout is the non-deletable baseline. JSON export/import transfers the browser-local state.

The collision and clearance labels are planning aids: they use oriented-rectangle geometry, height-aware checks for modular cabinets and TVs, and sampled opening sweeps. They do not replace site measurement or construction coordination.

## Cloudflare

Deploy the `dist` directory as a Cloudflare Pages static site, with no build command; pushes to `main` publish https://a7-space-studio.pages.dev/ after a successful deployment (the delay varies). The app has no server bindings or secrets. The A7 plan is public marketing material, not the owner's residence (confirmed 2026-09-11), so no access restriction is needed. Browser storage is per origin / device. Export and import JSON to transfer arrangements between deployments or devices.

## Scope and assumptions

A7 raster plan is the source of wall layout; A8 is furniture reference only. Dimensions are in metres internally; editor input is centimetres. The user-provided clear ceiling height is 3.00 m (2.79 m before 2026-09-24); assumed dimensions include wall thickness 0.12 m, door height 2.10 m, typical sill 0.90 m / glazing 1.50 m. Wall chains, column sizes, beam position, furniture dimensions, openings and swing directions require field verification. Model is an approximation, not construction documentation.

Procedural furniture uses locally generated material textures and geometric details. Finish and flooring textures are generated in background workers and cached in the browser (IndexedDB), so the model appears in its materials' median colours first and later visits skip the generation. There are no exact-brand assets: board finishes and floorings store only measured colours and a pattern type, and their grain is an approximation, not the real product. The view outside and the lift lobby are estimates from photos and the sales plan. Cabinet parts rebuild from dimensions. Exterior-boundary movement is constrained; interior collisions use 2D oriented rectangles and discrete samples of opening sweeps, then remain visible as editable drafts rather than being auto-resolved. Curtain animation is a simplified pleated opening, not cloth physics. Lighting is illustrative, not a lux calculation; daylight is shadowless sky light.

User states and up to 30 named scenarios are stored in localStorage with JSON import/export. The working layout is always retained; saved named scenarios can be removed. A saved layout that fails validation on load is set aside (up to three copies) before the fallback autosave can overwrite it, with a banner to download or discard it; autosave pauses if it cannot be set aside. The site also asks the browser for persistent storage. No account, cloud synchronisation or collaboration is implemented. Optional WebMCP tools are feature-detected.
